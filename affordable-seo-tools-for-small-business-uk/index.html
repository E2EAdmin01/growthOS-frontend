Affordable SEO Tools for Small Business UK: An Honest 2026 Guide

What's genuinely free, what the paid tools actually cost, and how to choose one you'll still be using in three months.

Published 22 September 2026 · Prices checked September 2026 · 10 min read

Most guides to affordable SEO tools for small business UK owners are written by the tools themselves, and quietly put their own product at the top of every list. We make one of these tools too, so we'll say that up front. RankActions is in this guide, alongside the others, and we'll tell you where other tools do a better job.

The honest starting point is this: most UK small businesses should spend nothing on SEO tools until they've used the free ones properly. When you do start paying, the right tool depends far less on features than on one question — will you actually open it every week?

Start with the free tools. Most businesses stop too early.

Before paying for anything, set these up. Between them they cover more than many paid subscriptions, and the first one is data no paid tool can give you.

Google Search Console

This is Google's own record of how your site performs in its search results: which searches you appear for, how often people see you, how often they click, and your average position. It also tells you whether your pages are indexed and flags technical problems. Every other tool either estimates this data or borrows it from here. It keeps 16 months of history, so set it up today even if you won't look at it for a while. Our guide to Google Search Console tools explains which reports are worth your time.

Google Business Profile

If customers visit you or you serve a local area, this matters as much as your website. It controls how you appear in Google Maps and the local results. Keep your opening hours, categories and photos current, and ask happy customers for reviews.

Bing Webmaster Tools

Bing's equivalent of Search Console. Its share of UK searches is small, but it takes minutes to set up because it can import your sites straight from Search Console.

Ahrefs Webmaster Tools

Ahrefs' free tier for sites you own. It audits your site for technical issues and shows which other websites link to you. It won't let you research competitors, but for your own site it's genuinely useful and costs nothing.

Screaming Frog SEO Spider (free version)

A desktop program that crawls your site the way a search engine does, finding broken links, redirect chains, missing titles and duplicate pages. The free version crawls up to 500 URLs, which covers most small business websites. It's thorough but technical: it tells you what's wrong, not what to do about it.

PageSpeed Insights

Google's free speed test. It reports your Core Web Vitals — loading speed, responsiveness and visual stability — using real visitor data where Google has enough of it.

The honest limit of the free stack

None of these tools will tell you what to do next. They show data and list issues, and leave the interpretation to you. For some people that's fine. For most small business owners, that's exactly where things stall.

Four jobs an SEO tool does

Rather than comparing feature lists, work out which of these jobs you actually need doing. Most small businesses need the first two badly, the third occasionally, and the fourth only once things are moving.

  1. Knowing what's happening. Which searches bring people to your site, and which pages are rising or falling. Search Console does this for free.
  2. Deciding what to fix. Turning data and audit results into a short list of changes worth making this week. This is where most people get stuck, and where the tools differ most.
  3. Finding what to write about. Keyword research: what people search for, how often, and how hard it would be to rank. This needs a paid database; the free options are thin.
  4. Checking whether it worked. Tracking positions over time so you can see whether a change made a difference, rather than guessing.

Screaming Frog

The best-value technical tool available, and a UK company. If your site is under 500 pages, the free version may be all you ever need for audits. The catch is that it assumes some technical knowledge: it will find 40 issues and leave you to decide which three matter. Our technical SEO guide covers what to prioritise.

Ubersuggest

The cheapest way into paid keyword research, and the lifetime option means you pay once. Its data is less complete than Semrush or Ahrefs, which matters most for niche UK searches with low volumes. For a small business wanting rough guidance on what to write about, that's often fine.

Ahrefs

Ahrefs' strength is its backlink data — who links to whom across the web. If studying competitors' links is central to your plans, it's hard to beat. For most small businesses, it's more than you need, and the free Webmaster Tools covers your own site.

Moz Pro

The friendliest of the established platforms, with some of the best learning material in the industry. The Starter plan is a reasonable option for one small site if you want to learn how SEO works rather than hand it off.

SE Ranking

Strong rank tracking and client-style reporting, which makes it popular with freelancers and small agencies. If you've seen it recommended as a cheap option in older articles, note that the lower-priced plan those articles describe no longer exists.

Semrush

The most complete platform here, covering keyword research, audits, competitor analysis, advertising research and more. That breadth is also the problem for a small business: you pay for a lot you won't use, and it takes real time to learn. It makes most sense once SEO is someone's regular job.

Which tool fits your situation

You've just launched a website

Set up Search Console, Google Business Profile and PageSpeed Insights, and spend nothing else yet. Search Console needs a few weeks of data before it tells you much. Use that time to make sure every page has a clear title and a clear purpose.

You're an established local service business

Your data is already in Search Console, and your biggest problem is almost certainly time, not information. A tool that turns that data into a short weekly to-do list will do more for you than a research platform you open once a month. Keep your Google Business Profile busy with reviews.

You want to learn SEO and do it yourself

Moz Pro Starter or Ubersuggest alongside the free tools, plus Screaming Frog's free version for technical checks. You'll learn more from interpreting the data yourself, and the costs are low.

SEO is someone's actual job

If a person on your team spends several hours a week on search, a full platform like Semrush, Ahrefs or SE Ranking starts to earn its price. Pick the one whose strengths match that person's work: links, content or tracking.

Hidden costs to check before you commit

  • Extra users. Many plans include one login. A second person often costs almost as much again.
  • Exchange rates and VAT. A price in dollars becomes a different figure in pounds every month.
  • Annual lock-in. Annual billing typically saves 17 to 20 percent, but most annual plans aren't refundable. Pay monthly until the tool has proved itself.
  • Usage limits. Credits, tracked keyword allowances and page limits vary widely. Check whether the entry plan covers your site before comparing headline prices.
  • Upgrade pressure. Several platforms put the most useful features one tier above the plan you'd naturally choose.

The cost nobody lists

The most expensive SEO tool is the one you pay for and don't open. Before buying, be honest about how much time you'll give it each week. If the answer is twenty minutes, choose the tool that gets the most done in twenty minutes, not the one with the longest feature list.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Search Console enough on its own?

For many UK small businesses it covers the most important data, because it's Google's own record of how your site performs, and it's free. What it doesn't do is tell you what to change. You either learn to read it, or add a tool that turns it into specific actions.

Do I need a backlink tool?

Not at first. Ahrefs Webmaster Tools shows the links pointing at a site you own for free. A paid backlink database mainly matters when you want to study competitors' links in depth, which is a later-stage job for most small businesses.

Should I pay monthly or annually?

Pay monthly until you've used the tool every week for at least two or three months. The annual saving is only a saving if you keep using it.

Are these prices charged in pounds?

Most of the big platforms list prices in US dollars, so what you pay moves with the exchange rate, and VAT may be added. Screaming Frog, a UK company, prices in pounds, as does RankActions.

Where to start this week

  1. Set up Google Search Console and Google Business Profile if you haven't already.
  2. Run a free crawl of your site with Screaming Frog and fix any broken links it finds.
  3. Decide honestly how much time you'll give SEO each week, then choose a tool that fits that time.
  4. Use free plans and trials with your own website before paying for anything.
