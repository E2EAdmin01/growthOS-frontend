SEO Tools Worth Paying For: Which Ones Actually Earn Their Cost?

A practical test for UK small businesses: what the free tools already cover, what paying adds, and when it makes sense to spend

By the RankActions team22 September 20265 min read
In this guide
  • What free tools already give you, and the three gaps paid tools fill
  • A four-question test to run before any subscription
  • Where paying clearly pays off, and where it usually doesn't

Working out which SEO tools worth paying for actually earn their cost is harder than it should be, because most of the advice comes from the companies selling them. We make an SEO tool too, so treat this as a buyer's guide written by a seller who is trying to be fair about it.

The short answer: for most UK small businesses, a paid tool is worth it only when it closes a gap the free tools genuinely leave open, and when someone will actually use it every week. Price matters much less than either of those.

What you already get for free

Before comparing subscriptions, it helps to be clear about how much the free tools already cover:

  • Google Search Console shows the searches you appear for, your clicks, impressions and average position, plus indexing and page experience problems. It's Google's own data, which no paid tool can see directly; they estimate it or borrow it from here.
  • PageSpeed Insights gives you Google's Core Web Vitals measurements for any page.
  • Screaming Frog's free version crawls up to 500 pages and finds broken links, redirect chains and missing titles.
  • Ahrefs Webmaster Tools audits a site you own and shows the links pointing to it.
  • Google Business Profile covers how you appear in Maps and local results.

That's a genuinely capable toolkit. Plenty of small businesses have ranked well using nothing else.

The three gaps paid tools fill

Paid SEO tools earn their place in three situations. If none of these applies to you, you probably don't need one yet.

1. Seeing beyond your own site

Search Console only shows your own performance. It can't tell you which searches a competitor ranks for, which sites link to them, or how many people search for a phrase you don't yet appear for. That outward-looking research needs a paid database such as Semrush, Ahrefs, Moz or Ubersuggest.

2. Saving time you'd otherwise spend interpreting data

The free tools produce data and lists of issues. Turning that into a decision about what to do this week takes time and some knowledge. Tools that summarise, prioritise or automate that step are selling time, not information. Whether that's worth paying for depends on what your time is worth and whether you'd otherwise do the work at all.

3. Tracking specific keywords reliably

Search Console reports an average position across every search, location and device. If you need to know exactly where you rank for a particular phrase in a particular place, or want daily checks, that's what a dedicated rank tracker is for. A four-question test before you subscribe

Run any tool you're considering through these questions. It should pass at least three.

  1. Which gap does it fill? Name the specific thing it does that your free tools don't. If you can't, stop there.
  2. Will someone open it every week? A tool used once a month rarely justifies a monthly subscription. Be honest about this one.
  3. Does it save more time than it costs? If your time is worth £40 an hour and the tool costs £100 a month, it needs to save you two and a half hours a month to break even, before counting any results it produces.
  4. Will its output lead to a change on your website? Reports that nobody acts on are the most common way SEO budgets get wasted. The value is in the changes, not the data.
A useful habit

Before paying, write down the three things you expect to do with the tool in its first month. Check the list after four weeks. If you haven't done them, cancel.

Where paying usually pays off

Keyword research before writing new content

If you publish regularly, knowing what people actually search for, and how competitive each phrase is, prevents months of writing articles nobody looks for. This is the clearest case for a paid research tool, and even a low-cost one like Ubersuggest covers the basics.

Competitor research when you're losing ground

If a competitor has overtaken you and you don't know why, a month of Semrush or Ahrefs will show you their strongest pages and where their links come from. This is often a one-off job rather than an ongoing need.

Turning data into a weekly routine

For business owners who have Search Console set up but never quite act on it, the most valuable thing a tool can do is reduce it to a short list of changes. This is the gap RankActions was built for. It's also the easiest one to test for free before committing.

Where it usually doesn't

  • An all-in-one platform for a small, stable site. If you have 15 pages that rarely change, you'll use a small fraction of Semrush or Ahrefs. Paying $130 or more a month for that is hard to justify.
  • A second tool that repeats the first. Two platforms that both audit your site and track rankings double the cost without doubling the value.
  • Anything bought before Search Console is set up. Get the free data flowing first. A paid tool can't fix the absence of the basics.
  • Annual plans before you know the tool. The annual discount, usually 17 to 20 percent, only saves money if you keep using it, and most annual plans aren't refundable.

The one-month approach

If you only need competitor or keyword research occasionally, you don't need a permanent subscription. Pay for one month of a research platform, do all of that research in a focused burst, save what you need, and cancel. Most of these tools bill monthly, so this is entirely legitimate, and it turns a large annual cost into an occasional one. Check each provider's cancellation terms first; some don't refund part-months.

Frequently asked questions

Is Semrush or Ahrefs worth it for a small business?

Usually only if you do regular competitor or keyword research, or someone on your team works on SEO every week. For a small, stable site, most of either platform would go unused. A one-month subscription for a focused research burst is often better value.

What is the single most useful paid SEO tool?

It depends which gap you have. For outward research, a keyword database. For technical checks on a large site, a paid Screaming Frog licence. For business owners who never act on their data, a tool that turns Search Console into a short weekly to-do list.

Can I do SEO with only free tools?

Yes. Google Search Console, Google Business Profile, PageSpeed Insights and the free versions of Screaming Frog and Ahrefs Webmaster Tools cover most of what a small business needs. What they don't do is tell you what to prioritise.
